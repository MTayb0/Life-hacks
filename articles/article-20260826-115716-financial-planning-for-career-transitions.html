Thinking of Resigning? Your Financial Checklist Before You Leap

2026-08-26 11:57 UTC · 💰 Money

The word 'resignation' is trending, and it’s no surprise. Whether spurred by a desire for a new challenge, a need for better work-life balance, or simply a feeling of 'it’s time,' many of us contemplate a career change at some point. But while the thought of a fresh start can be exhilarating, the financial implications require careful consideration. Before you draft that two-week notice, ensure your finances are ready for the leap.

Build Your Financial Runway

This is perhaps the most critical step. A 'financial runway' is essentially a buffer of savings that can cover your essential living expenses for a set period without income. Experts often recommend 3-6 months, but for a voluntary career transition, aiming for 6-12 months can provide invaluable peace of mind. This cushion allows you to take your time finding the right next opportunity, avoiding the pressure of taking the first job offer just to pay the bills.

Understand Your True Expenses

Before you know how much runway you need, you need to know what your runway needs to cover. Take a close look at your spending habits. Differentiate between 'needs' (housing, food, utilities, transportation, essential insurance) and 'wants' (dining out, entertainment, non-essential subscriptions). Can you temporarily reduce or eliminate some 'wants' during your transition period? Having a clear picture of your bare-minimum budget is empowering and helps you set a realistic savings goal.

Don't Forget Benefits

Beyond your salary, your current job likely offers benefits like health insurance, retirement contributions, and paid time off. When you resign, these all change. Research your options for health coverage (e.g., COBRA, marketplace plans) and understand the associated costs. Consider the impact on your retirement savings – will you roll over your 401(k) or let it sit? Factor these potential new expenses and lost employer contributions into your financial planning.

Outline Your Next Income Stream

Are you moving directly to another job? Starting a new venture? Taking a sabbatical? Each scenario has different financial implications. If you're job-hunting, consider how long it realistically takes to secure a new role in your industry. If you're freelancing or starting a business, project your initial income conservatively. Having a rough timeline and a conservative estimate for when and how new income will flow in is crucial for gauging the adequacy of your financial runway.

Resigning can be a powerful step towards personal and professional growth. By taking the time to plan your finances proactively, you can ensure your big move is met with confidence and control, not unexpected stress.
