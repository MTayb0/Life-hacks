Measles Alert: What You Need to Know After Universal Studios Hollywood Exposure

2026-08-06 07:35 UTC · 📰 News

In a reminder of persistent public health challenges, visitors to one of California’s most popular attractions, Universal Studios Hollywood, have recently been put on alert following a confirmed measles exposure.

The Los Angeles County Department of Public Health (LACDPH) issued a warning after an unvaccinated non-resident individual, confirmed to have measles, visited several high-traffic locations in Southern California while infectious. Among these locations was Universal Studios Hollywood, visited on January 16, potentially exposing thousands to the highly contagious virus.

Understanding the Measles Threat

Measles isn't just a childhood rash; it's a serious respiratory disease caused by a virus. It spreads through the air when an infected person coughs or sneezes. Symptoms typically appear 7-14 days after exposure and include fever, runny nose, cough, red eyes, and a sore throat, followed by a characteristic rash that spreads over the body.

While often considered an illness of the past, measles can lead to severe complications, especially in young children and adults, including pneumonia, encephalitis (swelling of the brain), and even death. It’s incredibly contagious, with the virus able to live in the air or on surfaces for up to two hours after an infected person leaves an area.

What to Do If You Were Potentially Exposed

For anyone who visited Universal Studios Hollywood on January 16, or other listed exposure sites, the LACDPH advises immediate action. The most crucial step is to check your vaccination status. The Measles, Mumps, and Rubella (MMR) vaccine is highly effective in preventing the disease. Two doses are recommended for full protection.

  • If you are unvaccinated or unsure of your vaccination status, contact your healthcare provider immediately.
  • Monitor for symptoms for up to 21 days after exposure. If symptoms develop, avoid public settings and contact your doctor before visiting a clinic or emergency room to prevent further spread.
  • Ensure infants, pregnant women, and individuals with weakened immune systems who may have been exposed receive immediate medical advice, as they are at higher risk for severe illness.

The Bigger Picture: Vaccination and Public Health

This recent alert underscores the ongoing importance of vaccination, not just for individual health but for community immunity. Measles outbreaks, once nearly eliminated in the U.S. due to widespread vaccination, have seen a resurgence in recent years, often linked to international travel and pockets of unvaccinated populations.

Public health officials continue to stress that vaccination remains the safest and most effective defense against measles, protecting both the vaccinated individual and vulnerable members of the community who cannot receive vaccines due to age or medical conditions.

Stay informed, stay vigilant, and ensure your vaccinations are up to date to help keep our communities safe from preventable diseases like measles.
