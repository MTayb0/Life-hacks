Lights Out? Your Essential Guide to Power Outage Preparedness

2026-08-08 19:06 UTC · 💡 How-To

One minute you're scrolling through the latest weather alerts for Omaha or planning your weekend in Houston, the next, everything goes dark. Power outages, whether caused by severe weather patterns tracked by NOAA or an unexpected grid issue, are a common annoyance that can quickly turn into a significant disruption. But with a little preparation, you can navigate these blackouts with confidence and keep your household running smoothly.

Assemble Your Emergency Kit

The foundation of any good outage plan is a well-stocked emergency kit. Keep these essentials in an easily accessible location:

  • Lighting: Flashlights and plenty of fresh batteries are paramount. Consider headlamps for hands-free convenience. Avoid candles as a primary light source due to fire risk.
  • Communication: A battery-powered or hand-crank radio can provide crucial updates from local authorities when cell towers are down. Make sure your cell phones, tablets, and laptops are fully charged, and invest in a portable power bank or car charger.
  • First Aid: A comprehensive first-aid kit for minor injuries and any necessary prescription medications.
  • Food & Water: Stock non-perishable food items that don't require cooking (e.g., canned goods, energy bars, dried fruit) and at least one gallon of water per person per day for drinking and sanitation.
  • Cash: ATMs and card readers won't work, so a small stash of cash can be a lifesaver.

Protect Your Home and Food

Beyond your kit, there are steps you can take around the house when the power goes out:

  • Food Safety: Keep refrigerator and freezer doors closed as much as possible. A full freezer can keep food frozen for about 48 hours, a half-full one for 24 hours. A fridge will keep food safe for about 4 hours. When in doubt, throw it out.
  • Unplug Electronics: Disconnect sensitive electronics like computers, TVs, and major appliances to protect them from power surges when electricity is restored.
  • Generator Safety: If you have a generator, never operate it indoors or in an attached garage. Generators produce carbon monoxide, a colorless, odorless, deadly gas. Keep it outdoors in a well-ventilated area away from windows.

Stay Safe and Informed

During an outage, safety is key:

  • Carbon Monoxide Detectors: Ensure you have working carbon monoxide detectors with battery backups.
  • Stay Connected: Follow local news channels or utility company social media (if you have phone battery) for updates on power restoration. Sign up for outage alerts from your utility provider in advance.
  • Community Support: Check on elderly neighbors or those with medical conditions who might need assistance.

While we can't control when the power goes out, we can control how prepared we are. A few simple steps can turn a stressful situation into a manageable inconvenience, giving you peace of mind no matter what Mother Nature (or the grid) throws your way.
