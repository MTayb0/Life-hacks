Banish the Yuck: How to Deep Clean and Refresh Your Pillows

2026-09-18 21:45 UTC · 💡 How-To

Your pillows are unsung heroes, silently supporting your head and neck night after night. But while they’re busy providing comfort, they’re also absorbing a cocktail of sweat, dead skin cells, oils, and dust mites. Yuck! Regular cleaning not only banishes these unwelcome guests but also extends the life of your pillows and significantly improves your sleep hygiene. Forget just changing pillowcases; it’s time for a deep clean!

Before You Start: Check the Label!

This is the golden rule for any textile cleaning. Most synthetic and down/feather pillows are machine washable, but memory foam, buckwheat, and some specialty pillows require a different approach. The care tag will tell you everything you need to know.

What You'll Need:

  • Mild laundry detergent
  • Washing machine (if applicable)
  • Dryer or a sunny, well-ventilated spot
  • 2-3 clean tennis balls (or dryer balls)

The Cleaning Process:

For Machine-Washable Pillows (Synthetic, Down, Feather)

Most standard bed pillows fall into this category. Here's how to give them a thorough wash:

  1. Load Two: To balance the washing machine, always wash two pillows at a time. This ensures an even clean and prevents the machine from becoming unbalanced.
  2. Gentle Cycle, Warm Water: Use a mild, liquid detergent – a little goes a long way. Set your machine to a gentle or delicate cycle with warm water. An extra rinse cycle is a great idea to ensure all detergent is removed.
  3. No Harsh Chemicals: Avoid bleach or harsh stain removers, especially for down or feather pillows, as they can damage the delicate fill.

For Spot-Clean Only Pillows (Memory Foam, Buckwheat, Latex)

These materials cannot be submerged in water, as it can break down their structure and lead to mildew. Instead:

  1. Vacuum Thoroughly: Use the upholstery attachment on your vacuum cleaner to remove surface dust and debris.
  2. Spot Clean Stains: If there are specific stains, dab them gently with a cloth lightly dampened with a solution of mild soap and water. Be careful not to saturate the material.
  3. Air Out: Place the pillow in a well-ventilated area, preferably outdoors on a sunny day, to air it out and remove odors.

The Drying Dilemma: Don’t Skip This Step!

Proper drying is arguably the most crucial part of pillow cleaning, especially for machine-washed pillows. If your pillows remain damp, they can become a breeding ground for mold and mildew.

  • Low Heat Tumble Dry: Pop your pillows into the dryer on a low heat setting. Add 2-3 clean tennis balls (placed inside socks to prevent dye transfer) or dryer balls. These will help fluff the pillows and break up any clumps, ensuring even drying.
  • Flip and Fluff: Periodically remove the pillows from the dryer, fluff them by hand, and feel for any damp spots.
  • Air Dry Finish: Once they feel dry to the touch, let them air dry for several hours, or even a full day, in a sunny, well-ventilated spot. Squeeze them regularly to check for hidden moisture.

Aim to deep clean your pillows every 3-6 months. Pair this routine with washable pillow protectors under your pillowcases, and you’ll enjoy fresher, cleaner, and healthier sleep for years to come. Sweet dreams!
